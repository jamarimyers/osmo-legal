# Osmo Privacy Policy

**Effective Date:** October 31, 2026  
**Last Updated:** October 31, 2026

## At a Glance

This summary covers the main points. The full policy below is what governs.

* **You need to be 18 or older** to use Osmo.
* **Connecting WHOOP is optional.** If you connect it, Osmo reads the WHOOP data you allow and uses only your own history to decide your state and recommendations.
* **Coaches see nothing about you until you turn on sharing.** Even then, they never see your raw WHOOP numbers, your journal, your reflections or your check-in answers. Section 7 lists exactly what they can see.
* **We do not sell your personal information,** and we do not use it for advertising. Osmo has no ads and does not track you across other companies' apps or websites.

* **You can delete your account at any time** from the Profile screen in the app. Deletion starts right away and cannot be undone.
* **Osmo is not medical care** and is not an emergency service. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 in the United States, or call your local emergency number.

## 1. Introduction

Stateframe Technologies, Inc. ("Stateframe," "we," "us" or "our") makes Osmo, an emotional performance app for athletes ("Osmo" or the "Services").

This Privacy Policy explains what information we collect through Osmo, how we use and share it, how long we keep it, how we protect it, and the choices and rights you have.

Osmo can connect to other services, such as WHOOP, if you choose to connect them. Stateframe is an independent company and is not affiliated with or endorsed by WHOOP, Inc.

## 2. Eligibility

Osmo is only for people who are 18 or older. By creating an account, you confirm that you are at least 18.

Osmo asks you to confirm that you are 18 or older when you first sign in. You do not need to enter your birth date to confirm. If you choose to add a date of birth to your profile, the app does not accept a date that would make you younger than 18.

Osmo is not directed to children under 18, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18. If we learn that someone under 18 has given us personal information, we will take reasonable steps to delete it and close the account, subject to applicable law. Section 11 explains the record we keep when an account is closed for this reason.

**Error monitoring.** Osmo uses Sentry to find and fix crashes and errors in the Osmo app and on Osmo's server. When something goes wrong, the app or the server sends Sentry an error report. A report can include:

* The error message and the place in Osmo's code where it happened  
* The app version and build  
* Technical details about your device, such as its model and operating system version  
* A short record of what happened just before the error, such as the screens that were open and the address of an Osmo server request that failed

Osmo also sends Sentry timing measurements for a sample of app and server activity, and records of app sessions (when the app was opened and whether the session ended in a crash), so we can see how reliable each release is. Sentry receives the network (IP) address of the device or server that sends a report, as any internet service does, and Osmo has set Sentry not to store it.

Osmo sets up Sentry to leave information out. In both the app and on our server, Sentry's option to attach personal information to reports automatically is turned off.

On our server, reports are filtered before they are sent:

* Email addresses, usernames, IP addresses, sign-in tokens and cookies are removed.  
* Fields that hold WHOOP values, emotional states, check-in answers, and written text such as journal entries, reflections and notes are removed.  
* The body of any request that carries your own writing or your sign-up details is dropped, and so is the query part of web addresses.  
* An error that comes from journal or Ozzy code keeps its type but loses its message text.  
* An internal account identifier is replaced with a keyed code, so reports can be grouped by account without showing the identifier. We can match that code to an account, so it is a pseudonym, not anonymous. (Section 11 explains how keyed codes work.)

Starting with the October 31, 2026 update to app version 1.0.3, the app also filters reports on your phone before they are sent. A new installation downloads that update the first time it opens with an internet connection, and uses it from the next launch. The app filters reports this way:

* Fields that hold WHOOP values, emotional states, check-in answers, and written text such as journal entries, reflections and notes are removed, and so are sign-in tokens and passwords.  
* The body of any request is dropped, and so is the query part of web addresses.  
* An error from a request that carries your own writing, your sign-up details or a team code, or an error whose message mentions the journal or Ozzy, keeps its type but loses its message text.  
* Release builds do not send the app's console output, and the labels of buttons and other items you tap are left out of the record of what happened before an error.

Two limits apply in the app. The app does not replace internal account identifiers with keyed codes, so an app report can include one. And if the app crashes in its native code (the parts that run directly on your phone's operating system rather than in the app's JavaScript), the crash is reported on the next launch without passing through this filtering.

Even with these steps, an error report can occasionally include other information related to what failed.

If we offer Ozzy or another AI feature, we will update this Privacy Policy and the list of service providers before it is available, and it will require a separate in-app acknowledgement.

## 7. Athlete Privacy and Coach Access

### What You Can See

You can see your Osmo information in the app, including supported raw WHOOP values such as HRV, resting heart rate, average and maximum heart rate, recovery percentage and sleep information.

You can download the WHOOP records Osmo holds about you from the app: Profile, then Settings, then WHOOP, then Download my WHOOP data. The download is a JSON file (a standard data format). It includes records Osmo still holds during the 30 days after you disconnect WHOOP. It does not include your WHOOP authorization tokens.

### What Coaches Can See

A coach sees information about you only after you turn on sharing with that coach. Sharing is off by default, and joining a team does not share anything by itself. You can turn sharing off at any time, and you then leave that coach's list. Once you share with a coach, that coach can see:

* Your name and email address  
* The day you were last active (never the time of day)  
* Whether you checked in today (yes or no)  
* Your check-in streak, including your streak for each of the last 14 days  
* Whether your latest summary is current or out of date  
* An alert when a streak of 5 or more days ends

If your choice to share is recorded in Osmo's consent log (its record of what you agreed to share; the in-app sharing screen tells you these labels come from your WHOOP data), the coach can also see labels that Osmo creates from your WHOOP data. They are labels only, never raw values:

* Your readiness label (Push today, Train smart today or Recover today), for today and for the last 14 days  
* A direction label for your HRV trend and a quality label for your sleep  
* An alert when you have been at Recover today for three days in a row

A coach sees only the first list if:

* You have not connected WHOOP  
* You have disconnected WHOOP. Labels stop reaching coaches as soon as you disconnect, even while the 30-day deletion is pending  
* Your sharing was set up before the consent log existed and you have not confirmed it again

If Osmo cannot read the consent log or your WHOOP connection, it withholds the WHOOP labels.

Coaches also see two kinds of team-level information:

* Counts of how many athletes on their team have joined, confirmed and are sharing. Athletes who are not sharing are never named.  
* Combined practice results: the average change in how athletes said they felt before and after a practice, grouped by type of practice and combined across the athletes sharing with that coach. No names and no individual scores are shown.

### What Coaches Never See

* Raw HRV, resting heart rate, recovery or sleep values  
* WHOOP authorization credentials or tokens  
* Your journal entries or reflection text  
* Your check-in answers, individual item responses, scores or exact check-in times  
* Which practices you did or when you did them  
* Your date of birth, push token or sign-in identifiers

Limits on coach access to raw WHOOP values are enforced by access controls in Osmo's database, not only by what the app's screens show.

## 8. WHOOP Authorization and Consent

Osmo receives nothing from WHOOP until you choose to connect your WHOOP account and complete WHOOP's authorization. The permissions Osmo requests set the limits of what it can receive.

You can disconnect WHOOP at any time. When you do:

* Osmo asks WHOOP to revoke its access right away and stops syncing.  
* Your raw WHOOP values are hidden in the app right away.  
* Osmo deletes the WHOOP records it holds 30 days after you disconnect. This includes WHOOP values copied into your check-in and practice records and into the text of daily briefings.  
* If you reconnect WHOOP within those 30 days, the deletion is called off and your history and your own baseline come back.

Readiness labels that Osmo created from your WHOOP data and stored in your practice records are not raw WHOOP values. Osmo keeps them until you delete your account.

Disconnecting WHOOP does not close your Osmo account, and you can keep using the parts of Osmo that do not need WHOOP data.

How long we keep WHOOP information is also subject to applicable law and to WHOOP's terms for apps that use its data.

## 11. Account Deletion and Eligibility Records

### Account Deletion

You can delete your Osmo account at any time from the Profile screen in the app, under Account. The app asks you to confirm twice before anything is deleted.

Deletion starts when you confirm. Osmo does not hold your account in a recovery window or keep your information while marking the account as deleted, and we cannot restore an account once it is deleted. A few steps depend on outside providers and can finish later or need action from you, as described below.

When you confirm, Osmo does the following, in this order:

1. Withdraws every Ozzy permission you have given and, if you turned on Ozzy's optional memory, asks the memory provider to erase it. That erasure can stay pending until the provider confirms it. If you never used Ozzy, there is nothing to withdraw.  
2. Asks WHOOP to revoke Osmo's access, so Osmo can no longer receive anything new from your WHOOP account.  
3. Deletes the WHOOP records Osmo stored, and clears WHOOP values out of your daily briefings, check-in records and practice records.  
4. Deletes your account record. Every record holding your information is linked to your account record and is deleted with it. That removes your access request, if you made one, your consent records, your email updates record, if you turned email updates on in the app, check-ins, journal entries, module progress and exercise completions, daily briefings, streak records, milestones, survey responses, emotional-context sessions, analytics events, your coach sharing links, and the Osmo summaries your coaches see.  
5. If you signed in with Apple, asks Apple to revoke Osmo's sign-in authorization and deletes the Apple token Osmo stored. If Apple cannot be reached, the app tells you, and you can remove Osmo yourself in your Apple Account settings.  
6. Deletes your login record.

If the request to WHOOP does not go through, deletion still continues and the app tells you plainly that the revocation failed.

Deleting your Osmo account does not delete information held by WHOOP, Apple, Google or another outside service. To delete information those services hold, contact them directly.

A few records about the deletion itself are kept outside Osmo's database. Our server logs record that an account was deleted, using an internal account identifier rather than your name or email address. If a step of the deletion fails, an error report carrying that internal identifier is sent to our error monitoring provider. Deleting your account does not remove these operational records. How long they are kept depends on the retention settings of the hosting and monitoring providers we use. Those providers may also keep backups that contain your information until the backups expire on their own schedules.

If you ever used Ozzy's optional memory with an outside provider, Osmo also keeps a purge receipt: a record that an erasure was requested and its status. The receipt has no name, email address or message text, only pseudonymous identifiers.

Disconnecting WHOOP is a separate action with a different result. Disconnecting stops new data right away and hides the WHOOP records Osmo already holds, and those records are deleted 30 days later. If you reconnect within 30 days, your history and your own baseline come back. Account deletion has no 30-day window: the WHOOP records are deleted when you confirm.

### Eligibility Records

Osmo accounts are for people 18 and older.

If you tell Osmo you are under 18, your account is deleted and Osmo writes a separate record noting that the email address used is not currently eligible to register. The purpose is narrow: it stops the same address from being used to sign up again right away.

**What the record contains.** A keyed cryptographic fingerprint of the email address, the reason the record was created, the date it was created and the date it expires. It does not contain your email address, your name or anything linking it to a deleted account.

**How the fingerprint works.** The address is run through a one-way calculation that uses a secret key held on Osmo's server. The same address always produces the same result, so Osmo can check whether an address used at sign-up matches an existing record. The calculation cannot be run backwards, so an address cannot be read out of a record. That does not make the record anonymous: anyone holding the secret key who already has a specific address can test whether it matches a record.

**How long it is kept.** An eligibility record is deleted automatically 365 days after it is created. Osmo ignores expired records when checking a sign-up, so an expired record cannot block registration even before it is removed.

**Who can see it.** These records are kept in a table that the app's ordinary access cannot reach. Coaches, athletic departments and other Osmo users cannot see them.

Because an eligibility record has no link to your account, deleting your account does not delete it. That is deliberate: the record has to outlast the account to work. If you believe a record was created in error, contact us using the information in Section 18.

## 12. Security

We use administrative, technical and organizational safeguards designed to protect information from unauthorized access, disclosure, change, loss or misuse. These include:

* WHOOP and Apple authorization tokens stored by Osmo are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM.  
* Osmo's database provider, Supabase, states that it encrypts all customer data at rest with AES-256 and in transit with TLS.  
* Information travels between the app, our server and our providers over encrypted connections.  
* Access to production systems is limited to authorized people and systems that need it.

No method of sending or storing information is completely secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security. If we learn of a security incident affecting your personal information, we will investigate it and notify you and others as the law requires.
